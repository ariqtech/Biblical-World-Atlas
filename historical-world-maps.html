Research integrity

Read a map in its historical context

An old map is reliable evidence that a particular mapmaker, publisher or community represented a place in a certain way. It is not automatically proof that every coastline, kingdom, voyage, mythical creature, biblical location or unexplored land depicted was real.

Dates: the archive records the date of the original cartographic work separately from modern digitization or later facsimiles. Projection: circular and polar maps are ordinary methods of presenting a spherical Earth on paper; the shape of a historical map does not establish a flat-Earth theory.

Connect maps with Scripture and research

Map archives can illuminate the history of place names, geographical knowledge, pilgrimage routes, trade routes and interpretations of biblical lands. A map from a later century is not direct evidence for the events described in an ancient biblical text.
