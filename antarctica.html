Geographical evidence and research references

Geographical observation

The continent

Antarctica is a continental landmass, largely covered by an ice sheet, surrounded by the Southern Ocean.

British Antarctic Survey ↗
Verified coastal features

Ice cliffs and shelves

Many Antarctic coastal ice shelves terminate in steep fronts, sometimes resembling walls in photographs. Such real features are not an encircling wall around Earth.

National Snow and Ice Data Center ↗
Satellite observations

Landsat Image Mosaic of Antarctica

USGS, NASA, and BAS assembled extensive satellite imagery into the LIMA mosaic. The USGS provides a viewer and public-domain mosaic imagery.

LIMA research project ↗
Primary legal text

The Antarctic Treaty

The 1959 treaty covers peaceful use, science and inspection. It does not describe a hidden ring of land or an ice wall surrounding a flat Earth.

Study the Antarctic ice-wall theory separately

Real coastal ice shelves are documented. The claim that Antarctica is an ice wall encircling a physically flat Earth is unsupported by credible geographical evidence. The proposed theory, and its relationship to historical maps and Bible interpretation, belongs only in the separate research section.
