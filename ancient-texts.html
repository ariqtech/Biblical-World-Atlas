Three distinct ancient works

Read the Books of Enoch

1 Enoch, 2 Enoch and 3 Enoch are different works preserved in different manuscript traditions. The Ethiopian Orthodox canon includes 1 Enoch; it does not treat all three identically. Use the reader to open each historical English edition, or follow the edition sources for comparison.

1 Enoch · Ethiopic / Geʽez tradition · Complete historical translation

1 Enoch — The Book of Enoch

Read R. H. Charles's English translation (1917), published as an open public-domain text by Project Gutenberg. Includes the main sections of 1 Enoch.

Biblical connections: Jude 1:14–15 ↔ 1 Enoch 1:9 (close quotation); Genesis 6:1–4 ↔ Watchers traditions (related narrative).

2 Enoch · Slavonic tradition · Complete historical edition

2 Enoch — Secrets of Enoch

Read W. R. Morfill's translation, edited by R. H. Charles (1896), with chapter-by-chapter navigation at Scriptura.

Biblical connections: Genesis 5:21–24 (Enoch's life and disappearance); these are shared narrative themes, not direct quotations.

3 Enoch · Hebrew / Hekhalot tradition · Historical edition

3 Enoch — Hebrew Book of Enoch

Read the historical English wording of Hugo Odeberg’s 1928 translation, including the lettered sections. The printed edition preserves the scholarly apparatus.

Biblical connections: Genesis 5:24 (Enoch); Ezekiel 1 (visions) are thematic parallels, not proof of identical origin.

Book of Jasher / Sefer ha-Yashar

Read the later 1887 published translation. Its identity is not established as the lost Book of Jashar named in the Bible.

Compare: Joshua 10:13; 2 Samuel 1:18, which mention an earlier work.

Fragmentary ancient writings

Book of Giants

Preserved in manuscript fragments, including some from Qumran. There is no intact complete ancient Book of Giants to display; editions reconstruct fragments to differing degrees.

Compare: Genesis 6:1–4; 1 Enoch 6–11.
